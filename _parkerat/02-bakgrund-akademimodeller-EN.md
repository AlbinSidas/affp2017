# Youth Development at Åtvidabergs FF
## An organisational model, and how to get there from here

*Third document in the set. `football-training-areas-taxonomy.md` is the reference library; `traningsplan-9-aringar.md` is one team's application; this is the club.*

---

## 1. Why this is not optional for ÅFF specifically

Start here, because this is the argument that moves a board, and the pedagogy is not.

Åtvidaberg came up from Division 2 in 2025 and is playing Ettan Södra in 2026 [1][2]. The trajectory has turned. But the structural facts have not: this is a club in a small municipality, competing in a division where several opponents draw on catchments many times larger, with no realistic route to buying its way upward.

There are only two ways a club of this size sustains itself above Division 2. It produces players, or it doesn't survive at that level. Åtvidaberg has done the first before — the 1972 and 1973 Allsvenskan titles were won by a small-town club whose model was producing and keeping its own. **This is a restoration argument, not an innovation argument.** That framing matters when you pitch it, because it makes the proposal conservative rather than radical, which is what you want when you are relatively new and asking for structural change.

The second thing to be clear about, internally, is what the binding constraint actually is.

> **For a club in this catchment, the limiting factor is not coaching quality. It is the number of children still playing at 15.**

If the club has forty boys in an age group at nine and eleven at fifteen, no amount of coaching sophistication in between will produce a first-team player, because the pool is gone. If it has forty at nine and thirty at fifteen, mediocre coaching will still produce someone. **Retention is the strategy. Quality of coaching is a lever on retention, not an end in itself.**

Hold onto that, because it disciplines every decision below — and it is the reason the answer to your specialisation question is not the obvious one.

## 2. Your question: how do the large academies handle this?

You framed it as cross-functional versus specialised: most clubs have one coach doing everything for one team, whereas specialists serving many teams would allow better-targeted, lower-cost coaching. That is a good instinct and it is roughly what big academies do — but they specialise along **four separate axes**, and they cost wildly different amounts.

### Axis 1 — Age phase (horizontal specialisation)

Academies organise coaches by **development phase**, not by cohort. Barcelona runs coordinators per stage: one for grassroots football (roughly U8–U12), one for youth (U14–U16), one for the transition stage into the B team [3]. English academies use the same three-phase structure — Foundation, Youth Development, Professional Development [4].

The consequence is subtle and it is the most important thing in this document. **An academy Foundation Phase coach coaches nine-year-olds every year for a decade.** They run that age group ten times. They get genuinely, compoundingly good at it.

A grassroots parent-coach does the opposite. They follow their own child from 6 to 15. By the time they have worked out how to coach nine-year-olds, they are coaching twelve-year-olds and are a beginner again. Over ten years the club accumulates almost no transferable expertise, because every coach is permanently one year behind their own learning curve, and when their child stops playing, all of it leaves the building.

**This costs nothing to fix.** It is not a budget decision. It is a decision about who coaches whom.

### Axis 2 — Discipline (functional specialisation)

Goalkeeper coaches, strength and conditioning, physiotherapy, psychology, analysts, nutrition. Barcelona's "performance area" contains exactly this list [3]. This is where academy money goes, and it is the axis you cannot compete on.

With one exception. **Goalkeeping is not optional even at grassroots**, because it is the one position most youth coaches genuinely cannot coach — most are outfield players by background, and the standard failure mode is to separate the keeper and keep them busy rather than coach them [5]. SvFF runs a dedicated goalkeeper track (SvFF Mv D, Mv C) through the districts [6].

### Axis 3 — Playing position

Real but late. Academies generally hold off on fixing positional roles until around U14, and deliberately keep players experiencing multiple positions before that [7]. Not relevant to ÅFF below the junior teams.

### Axis 4 — Methodology and coherence

The one people miss. Barcelona has a **methodology department whose job is to ensure the game model is consistent across all categories** [3]. Ajax runs an academy technical director reporting directly to the first-team sporting director [3]. Neither of these people coaches a team. Their output is *coherence* — the thing Swedish football calls the **röd tråd**.

This is a role, not a resource. It is affordable.

## 3. What actually transfers to a club with limited resources

Here is the honest ranking by value per krona. It does not look like the academy org chart.

| Rank | Intervention | Cost | Why it ranks here |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Age-phase specialisation** — coaches own a phase, not a cohort | Zero kronor. One difficult cultural conversation. | Compounds annually. Nothing else in this table compounds. |
| **2** | **A written spelarutbildningsplan** — 4–6 pages, club-specific | Time only | Turns individual coach quality into club property. Survives coach turnover. |
| **3** | **Peer observation between coaches** | Zero | Borrowed from UEFA C's *mikrogruppsbesök* format [8]. The cheapest coach development that exists. |
| **4** | **One club-wide goalkeeper coach** | One person, possibly part-paid | The only discipline specialisation with a genuine gap at grassroots [5]. |
| **5** | **One methodology / coach-development lead** | Ideally part-paid | The Barça methodology function at 1/1000th the scale. Coaches the coaches; runs no team. |
| **6** | **Shared session bank** | Time | Removes the weekly blank-page problem, which is the actual reason volunteer sessions are poor. |
| **7** | **Common warm-up across the club** (FIFA 11+ Kids) | Zero | Injury prevention, coordination work, and visible shared identity in one move [9][10]. |
| ... | *S&C, psychology, analysis, nutrition* | Real money | Later. And when it comes, buy it as **coach education**, not as direct service to children. |

### The headline

> **Specialise the support, not the delivery.**

Put the specialists behind the coaches, not in front of the children. One goalkeeper coach and one methodology lead, multiplying through twenty volunteer team coaches, will move the club further than four specialists running sessions directly — and it is logistically survivable, which the alternative is not.

### And the counter-argument you need to hear

Your instinct — specialists deliver better-targeted coaching — is right from roughly 12 upward. **Below that it is wrong, and applying it would damage the club.**

At nine, the largest performance variable is not the technical quality of the input. It is whether a consistent, trusted adult shows up every week and the child wants to come back. SvFF's whole framework for these ages is built on *fotbollsglädje*, variety, and a child-rights perspective, and it warns explicitly that intense, elite-directed environments produce high expectations, heavy pressure, a small yield of good players, and a lot of dropout [11].

Read that against Section 1. **The intervention that improves session quality by 20% while raising dropout by 15% is a net loss for this club.** A rotating cast of specialists is exactly the intervention that risks doing that. So:

- **Foundation phase (5v5, 7v7):** one consistent coach per group. Specialise the *support* they receive.
- **From 9v9 upward:** the case for specialist input strengthens, and the goalkeeper coach becomes essential rather than desirable.

## 4. Proposed model for ÅFF

Roles, most of them held by people already in the club.

```
                    Sportchef / styrelse
                             |
              Fotbollsutvecklare, ungdom  ← the key role
              (owns the röd tråd; coaches the coaches; runs no team)
                             |
        ┌────────────────────┼────────────────────┐
   Fasansvarig           Fasansvarig          Fasansvarig
   5 mot 5 (6–9)        7 mot 7 (10–12)      9v9 / 11v11 (13+)
        |                    |                    |
   team coaches         team coaches         team coaches

              Målvaktstränare  ── serves all phases ──
```

**Fotbollsutvecklare, ungdom.** One person. Ideally part-paid, even at a token level, because unpaid strategic roles decay. Owns the club SUP, runs the coach meetings, does the peer-observation rounds, holds the session bank, is the point of contact with Östergötlands FF. Does *not* coach a team — that is the whole point, and it is the hardest part to sell.

**Fasansvarig × 3.** Existing coaches who take a coordination role for their phase. They keep coaching. They convene their phase's coaches once a term, agree the block themes, and induct new coaches. This is the Barcelona stage-coordinator model [3] shrunk to a volunteer club.

**Målvaktstränare.** One person, club-wide, from 9v9 upward. Route them through SvFF Mv D [6].

**Team coaches.** Everyone else. Unchanged in workload, better supported.

### The five mechanisms

1. **Club SUP** — 4–6 pages. What we train at each spelform, how we run a session, how we behave on match day, what we promise parents.
2. **Block themes per phase** — each phase agrees 3–4 week themes for the season. Every 7v7 team works the same theme in the same window. Coaches then help each other instead of working alone.
3. **Mikrogruppsbesök** — every coach watches two other sessions per season and hosts one. Structured, low-stakes, no evaluation. Copied from UEFA C [8].
4. **Coach education targets** — every youth coach holds SvFF D; every coach from 7v7 upward works toward UEFA C, which SvFF's own plan recommends from that spelform [12][13].
5. **Common warm-up** — FIFA 11+ Kids across every team in barnfotboll [9][10].

## 5. Rollout

Three years. Do not compress it.

### Phase 0 — now to spring 2027: prove it, don't propose it

**Propose nothing yet.** Run the model in your own group for one full season. Keep the session log. Track retention, coach hours, what worked. Do SvFF D.

At the end you will have something no document can substitute for: *"Here is what we did with the 2016s, here is what happened, here is what it cost."* One working example from inside the club beats any amount of imported theory, and it removes the only real objection to you personally — that you are new to this.

### Phase 1 — 2027: one phase

Ask for **one phase**, not the club. Almost certainly 7 mot 7, because it is where the coaching problem is most obvious and where SvFF already recommends UEFA C.

- Write the SUP for that phase only
- Appoint one fasansvarig
- Agree block themes across the phase's teams
- Run the first round of mikrogruppsbesök
- Get every coach in the phase onto SvFF D

Frame 2027 as a **startår** — a starting year in which the club tests, evaluates and develops the approach together, coaches get support and education, and parents meet new routines gradually. Melleruds IF used exactly this framing when launching their own SUP in 2026 [14], and it is worth copying, because it lowers the stakes and makes the first year's inevitable mess a feature rather than a failure.

### Phase 2 — 2028: the club

- Extend the SUP to all phases
- Appoint the remaining fasansvariga
- Appoint the fotbollsutvecklare, with at least token payment
- Recruit and train the club goalkeeper coach
- First full retention measurement across cohorts

### Phase 3 — 2029 onward: connect it upward

- Formal link between the junior teams and the A-squad, in the Ajax vertical-integration sense [3]
- Annual review of the SUP
- Only now consider paid specialist support in other disciplines

## 6. How to pitch it

The technical content is the easy part. This section is the one that determines whether any of it happens.

**Do not hand them the taxonomy document.** It is a reference library, and presented as a proposal it will read as an outsider arriving with a theory. Keep it as your source material and show it to nobody except, eventually, the fotbollsutvecklare.

**Write 4–6 pages in Swedish, in SvFF's vocabulary.** *Spelarutbildningsplan. Röd tråd. Spelformer. Allsidighet. Fotbollsglädje.* Not four-corner model, not constraints-led approach, not Foundation Phase. This is not cosmetic. SvFF's Spelarutbildningsplan is explicitly designed as a tool for clubs to build their own plan, which together with a club policy forms the röd tråd — the stated ambition is going *från policy till praktik* [11]. You are therefore not importing a foreign model. **You are implementing the Swedish FA's existing framework, which the club is arguably already supposed to be doing.** That is a much stronger position to argue from, and it is true.

**Lead with Section 1, not the pedagogy.** Board members are not moved by scanning research. They are moved by: *a club in Ettan with our catchment cannot buy players; we have to produce them; we used to be very good at this.*

**Ask, don't announce.** Find whoever holds sporting responsibility for youth and ask what they think the biggest problem is. There is a reasonable chance they already know, already agree, and have simply lacked anyone willing to do the work. Coming in with a finished plan wastes that.

**Ask for less than you want.** One phase. One season. One page of commitments.

**Bring your own numbers.** Your session log and your retention figures, from your own group, in your own club. Not literature.

## 7. What to measure

Four numbers. Reviewed annually. Nothing else.

| Metric | Why |
|---|---|
| **Players per birth-year cohort at 9, 12, 15** | The single most important number in this document. Everything else is instrumental to it. |
| **Coaches holding SvFF D / UEFA C** | Direct, cheap proxy for coaching quality. |
| **Coach retention year over year** | Coach churn destroys age-phase specialisation faster than anything else. |
| **Players progressing from own youth to junior / A-squad** | The actual output. Ten-year lag. Track it anyway, because it is the number that keeps the board committed. |

## 8. Risks, honestly

**Elite drift.** The largest risk, and it is created by exactly this kind of proposal. Professionalising youth work can slide into selecting, streaming, and pressure, at ages where SvFF's framework and the evidence both say it produces dropout and a poor yield [11]. Write the anti-elite commitments into the SUP explicitly — everyone plays, no tables in barnfotboll, other sports encouraged — so that the plan constrains the club rather than licensing it.

**The document nobody uses.** The default failure mode of every club SUP ever written. Prevented only by keeping it short and attaching it to a mechanism: block themes, coach meetings, peer observation. A plan with no meeting attached to it is decoration.

**Volunteer burnout.** You are proposing additional work for unpaid people. Every mechanism in Section 4 must *reduce* a coach's Tuesday-evening workload, not increase it. The session bank is the clearest example — it should save a coach thirty minutes a week. If a mechanism doesn't pay for itself in coach time, cut it.

**Coach turnover.** Age-phase specialisation only compounds if coaches stay. Which means the fotbollsutvecklare's real job is retaining coaches, not instructing them.

**The parent-coach problem.** Age-phase specialisation asks a parent to coach a group that is not their child's. Some will refuse, and they are not being unreasonable. Introduce it gradually, for new coaches first, and never as a rule imposed on existing volunteers.

**You.** You are relatively new, without a playing background, proposing structural change. That is a real vulnerability and pretending otherwise helps nobody. It is also entirely survivable, on one condition: **be the person who did the work in their own team first.** Phase 0 exists for this reason. Do not skip it.
