# Background — why the spelidé looks the way it does
## Reference material behind `00-BAS/01-spelide.md`

*English. Background and source material, not an operative document. The club's
actual playing identity is the Swedish document in `00-BAS`. Read this only if you
need the reasoning behind it — for example when the spelidé is revised.*

---

## 0. The design problem

A club-wide way of playing raises the standard and lets players move between age groups
without relearning football. It is the single highest-value thing a club of ÅFF's size can
build, because it is the one form of quality that survives coach turnover.

But the Spanish example most clubs reach for teaches a different lesson than the popular
version of it does, and getting this wrong is the most common way clubs waste five years.

### What actually travelled from La Masia to the World Cup

Spain won the 2026 World Cup on 19 July, beating Argentina 1–0 in extra time, conceding one goal across the entire tournament. Sixteen years after 2010. That looks like proof that a single methodology, applied from age seven, produces champions two decades later.

Look closer at what stayed the same and what didn't.

**What changed — the style.** The 2008–2012 side was defined by tiki-taka: patient central circuits, short passing, possession as an end in itself. By 2022 it had calcified. Spain had 77% possession against Morocco, completed over a thousand passes, and managed one shot on target in 120 minutes before going out. Luis de la Fuente then deliberately broke it. Against Croatia at Euro 2024, Spain had less possession than their opponent for the first time in 136 matches. Rodri put the new intention plainly: be more direct, and use possession always to damage the opponent. Rodri also said something a club writing a philosophy document should tattoo on the wall: he was not the guardian of any philosophy, and you adapt against every team. The wingers — Yamal and Williams — were given width and licence to go 1v1, and that is where the goals came from.

**What stayed the same — the player.** Technically secure under pressure. Understands space without being told. Comfortable receiving with an opponent behind. Able to make decisions quickly. Those qualities were built in the academies, and they were as useful to a direct, wing-focused 2024 side and a defensively suffocating 2026 side as they were to the 2010 one.

> **The conclusion: standardise the player and the principles. Do not standardise the style.**
>
> A style locks you into one answer and ages badly. A player profile and a set of principles produce footballers who fit whatever the game becomes.

### The failure mode you must design against

When mid-table clubs copied Barcelona in the early 2010s, they mostly produced **sterile possession** — horizontal, low-risk passing circuits, high control statistics, no penetration and no shots. Chelsea spent heavily trying to recreate La Masia at Cobham and produced very few first-team players. Arsène Wenger's name for it was sterile domination. Gary Neville's diagnosis was blunter: what actually made Barcelona exceptional was world-class dribbling in the final third and Lionel Messi, not the passing itself.

It gets worse at youth level, and Barcelona has been criticised for it internally. When the dominant coaching phrase becomes *touch it straight away, don't carry it*, players stop learning to dribble — and without dribbling, nobody creates the overload that breaks a compact defence. There is a documented tendency for youth coaches to discourage dribbling precisely because a dribbling player leaves the position they have been assigned. That is positional play badly taught, and it is the exact thing a volunteer-coached club will produce if it writes "we play possession football" and stops there.

**Predict the failure and design against it.** With volunteer coaches and ordinary technical players, "we play possession" reliably becomes: pass sideways, never dribble, don't lose it, be safe. That produces obedient nine-year-olds and no footballers.

So the model below has an anti-sterility clause built into its second pillar, and the club measures penetration rather than possession. That is the difference between a copy and a model.

## 1. The model in three words

Everything in this document is elaboration of three words. A nine-year-old can hold three words. A volunteer coach can hold three words on a wet Tuesday. Nothing longer survives contact with a club.

# BOLL · MOD · PRESS

**BOLL — vi vill ha bollen.**
We try to keep it. We play out from the back. We do not clear the ball as a first choice at any age. Losing the ball while trying to play is acceptable; refusing to try is not.

**MOD — vi vågar.**
We play forward when forward is on. We take the 1v1. We dribble. Possession exists to hurt the opponent, not to avoid risk. *This is the pillar that stops us becoming a bad copy, and it is non-negotiable.*

**PRESS — vi tar den tillbaka direkt.**
When we lose it, the nearest player presses immediately and everyone else squeezes. We defend forward, not backward.

The pillars are deliberately in that order. Ball gives you the platform. Courage is what you do with it. Press is how you get it back.

**The name — *Kopparspelet*** — is local and ownable rather than borrowed. A model with a Spanish name will always be somebody else's.

## 2. The player we are building

Before the tactics. This is the actual output of the club, and it is what stays constant while styles change.

**En ÅFF-spelare:**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Är trygg med bollen under press** | Can receive with an opponent behind and still have options |
| **Vågar gå förbi en motståndare** | Will take a 1v1 anywhere in the attacking half, and is never criticised for trying |
| **Kan spela med båda fötterna** | Not equally — but usably |
| **Tittar innan hen får bollen** | Scanning is a habit, not an instruction |
| **Förstår ytor** | Knows where to stand without being told each time |
| **Jagar bollen när vi tappat den** | Presses by reflex |
| **Kan spela på flera positioner** | Because roles change and because it builds understanding |

Every session in the club, at every age, should be defensible as building one of those seven things. If it isn't, cut it.

## 3. The positional spine — the thing that actually solves your problem

The stated goal was making it easier to move players between age brackets. **This section is the mechanism.** Not the principles, not the philosophy — this.

### Persistent numbers

Every position in the club has a number that means the same thing at every spelform, from 5 mot 5 to the A-team. A *sexa* is a sexa at nine and at nineteen.

| Nr | Roll | Kärnuppgift |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Målvakt | Ytterst back, först i anfallet |
| **2 / 3** | Ytterbackar (höger / vänster) | Bredd djupt, understöd |
| **4 / 5** | Mittbackar | Spela ut, försvara ytan bakom |
| **6** | Innermittfältare, defensiv | Ankaret. Alltid spelbar bakom bollen |
| **8** | Innermittfältare | Länken. Löper framåt |
| **10** | Offensiv mittfältare | Mellan linjerna |
| **7 / 11** | Ytterforwards (höger / vänster) | Bredd högt. **Går 1 mot 1** |
| **9** | Anfallare | Referenspunkt högst upp |

### Formation progression

The shapes are chosen so that the reference points never change. A boy who played the front point of the 5v5 diamond has already been a nine. A boy who played its base has already been a six.

| Spelform | Ålder | Formation | Numren som används |
|---|---|---|---|
| **5 mot 5** | 6–9 | **1-2-1 (diamant)** | 6 · 2 · 3 · 9 |
| **7 mot 7** | 10–12 | **2-3-1** | 2 · 3 · 6 · 7 · 11 · 9 |
| **9 mot 9** | 13–14 | **3-2-3** | 2 · 4 · 3 · 6 · 8 · 7 · 9 · 11 |
| **11 mot 11** | 15+ | **4-3-3** | Alla |

What is preserved at every step: **someone behind the ball in the middle (6), width high on both sides (7/11), one furthest-forward reference (9), and a back line that plays out.** Those four things are the whole spine. Everything else is detail.

**Why the diamond at 5 mot 5.** A 2-2 teaches children to play in flat lines and gives them no central option. The diamond gives depth, width, a player behind the ball and a player in front — the same four relationships they will use for the next decade, in the simplest possible form. It concedes more goals than a 2-2. Concede them.

**Critical rule for barnfotboll:** the numbers are a shared language, **not** fixed positions. Every child plays every number across a season. In barnfotboll nobody is a six; children take turns *being* the six. The vocabulary is what transfers, not the role.

## 4. Principles by moment of the game

Mapped to the four moments in the taxonomy document (Domain B2), so the two documents use the same skeleton.

### 4.1 Vi har bollen

1. **Öppna planen direkt.** Width and depth the instant we win it. Make the pitch as big as possible.
2. **Spela ut bakifrån.** Playing out is the default at every age. Clearing is a last resort, not a safe option.
3. **Sexan är alltid spelbar.** There is always a central option behind the ball.
4. **Hitta den fria mannen.** If two opponents press one of ours, someone is free. Find them — often via a third player.
5. **Vänd spelet när det är stängt.** Blocked on one side means switch, not force.
6. **Våga gå förbi.** *In the attacking half, 1v1 is an obligation, not a permission.*

### 4.2 Vi har inte bollen

1. **Press på bollhållaren** — the nearest player closes, doesn't dive in.
2. **Täck bakom** — the second defender covers the first.
3. **Var kompakt** — squeeze the distance between our lines.
4. **Styr åt ett håll** — force play one way and load that side.

### 4.3 Vi tappar bollen

1. **Fem sekunder.** Immediate counter-press by whoever is closest.
2. **Övriga stänger inåt** while the press happens.
3. Failing that, drop and reorganise — no panic.

### 4.4 Vi vinner bollen

1. **Titta framåt först.** First look is always forward.
2. **Framåt om det finns, behåll om det inte finns.** Direct if it's on, secure it if it isn't. Both are correct answers.

## 5. What is taught at each age

The most important table in this document. A model that teaches the same thing at nine and nineteen is not a model — it is an imposition.

| Spelform | Vad vi lär ut | Vad vi INTE gör |
|---|---|---|
| **5 mot 5**<br>6–9 år | **BOLL och MOD only.** "Sprid ut er." Position numbers used as words. Everyone plays everywhere. Dribbling praised constantly. Diamond as a starting shape only. | No pressing scheme. No fixed positions. No tactical instruction during matches. No touch limits. |
| **7 mot 7**<br>10–12 år | The three words properly. Formation as reference points. Playing out from the back. Width from 7 and 11. Pressing the ball carrier. Introduce three corridors. | No cover shadows, no pressing triggers, no rotations, no opponent-specific plans. |
| **9 mot 9**<br>13–14 år | Full principles across all four moments. Five corridors. Unit work (back three, midfield two, front three). Counter-press. First position specialisation, still with rotation. | No rigid roles. No sacrificing individual development for results. |
| **11 mot 11**<br>15+ | The complete model. Opponent adaptation. Set pieces. Game management. | — |

**Read the first row again.** At 5 mot 5, the game model is *two words and a starting shape*. That is the correct amount, and a well-run Foundation Phase session already delivers it — dribbling, 1v1, courage on the ball.

## 6. Anti-sterility safeguards

Written into the model as binding rules, because this is the failure the model is most likely to suffer.

1. **In barnfotboll, an attempted dribble is never a mistake.** A player who tries to beat an opponent and loses the ball is coached positively. Every time. Without exception.
2. **No touch restrictions in matches below 9 mot 9.** Two-touch rules manufacture risk aversion. Use them in training exercises if you like; never in a game.
3. **The 1v1 obligation.** In the attacking half, a player who is 1v1 is expected to take the opponent on. Choosing the safe backward pass instead is the thing we correct — not the failed dribble.
4. **Every player must be able to dribble out of pressure**, not only pass out of it. Both are taught.
5. **Possession percentage is never measured, discussed, or celebrated at any level of this club.** We measure penetration: passes into the final third, entries into the box, 1v1 attempts, shots. **If ÅFF ever starts congratulating itself on possession statistics, the model has failed.**

That fifth rule is the sharpest tool in this document. Keep it.

## 7. How coaches learn and apply it

A game model lives or dies on whether a volunteer can use it on a Tuesday. Three levels of documentation, and no more.

**Nivå 1 — En sida.** BOLL · MOD · PRESS, the four moments, the player profile. Laminated, on the clubhouse wall, in every parent pack, in every player's bag. Everyone in the club has this.

**Nivå 2 — En sida per spelform.** Formation diagram, the numbers used, what we teach at this age, what we don't, three example exercises. Four pages total for the whole club. Coaches have this.

**Nivå 3 — This document.** The fotbollsutvecklare and the fasansvariga have it. Nobody else needs it.

### The one habit to import from La Masia

Not tiki-taka. **The rondo.**

At La Masia, every session begins the same way — a circle, one or two in the middle, ball moved around them, often one touch. It runs from age seven to the first team. The methodology coaches describe it as work on body position and ball circulation, growing into work on positioning and understanding space as players get older.

Adopt this from **7 mot 7 upward**: every ÅFF session begins with a rondo. Five minutes. It costs nothing, requires no coaching expertise to set up, delivers enormous touch volume under pressure, and — the actual point — it means a boy moving from the 2014s to the 2013s walks into a session that starts exactly the way his old one did.

From 9 mot 9, progress to the **positional rondo** (*rondo de posición*): the same exercise with zones, so players must hold a position rather than chase the ball. That is juego de posición in its smallest teachable unit, and it is where the model becomes tactical.

**At 5 mot 5, no rondos.** Nine-year-olds in a passing circle is standing still. Their version is the arrival activity with a ball each.

### Mechanisms that keep it alive

- **Block themes tied to principles.** Each phase picks its blocks from Section 4. The whole phase works the same principle in the same window.
- **Common vocabulary policed gently.** In mikrogruppsbesök, one thing the observer notes: *is the coach using the club's words?* Not whether the session was good — whether the language was shared.
- **The three words said out loud every session.** Cheap, and it is how a culture actually forms.

## 8. The A-team problem

Every club game model dies in the same place: the A-team coach doesn't play that way, so the youth model is quietly revealed as decoration.

You cannot solve this by requiring an Ettan coach to play a fixed formation. He has to win games, and a formation mandate is both unreasonable and bad football. Two things you can do:

1. **The principles apply upward; the formation does not.** The A-team is expected to want the ball, play forward with courage, and press on losing it. Whether that comes in a 4-3-3 is his business.
2. **The model is owned by the club, not the coach.** The sportchef and fotbollsutvecklare own it. It survives coach changes. And philosophy fit becomes a stated criterion when the club appoints — not the only one, but a stated one.

Be realistic about the timeline. A nine-year-old today reaches the A-team squad in the mid-2030s at the earliest. **The model's payoff is a decade out.** Anyone who needs it to justify itself sooner will kill it. Say that out loud when you present it, because it inoculates against the year-three "is this working?" conversation.

---

## 9. On national ambition

Sweden already has a national framework — Spelarutbildningsplanen and Fotbollens spela, lek och lär — and it is a good one. The realistic contribution is not a competing model. It is being the club that demonstrates a coherent spelidé can be implemented by volunteers in a small town, and then being generous with what it learns: sharing the documents with Östergötlands FF, hosting other clubs, letting people copy.

That is a genuinely achievable ambition and it is worth more than a proposal nobody asked for. Small clubs change national football by being conspicuously good, not by writing to the federation.

---

## 10. Honest limits

- **La Masia is not reproducible and it wasn't purely methodological.** It had extraordinary intake, a coherent first-team pathway, and Messi. Neville's point stands: the philosophy needed a player like that to look like genius rather than sideways passing.
- **Even Barcelona got parts of it wrong.** Internal criticism has pointed at players who couldn't head the ball and were afraid to jump, and at a "touch it straight away" culture that suppressed dribbling. If they can drift into that, so can anyone.
- **Spain's model changed.** Twice, visibly, between 2010 and 2026. Anything written in 2026 will look dated by 2036. Write principles, review them every three years, and expect to be wrong about the details.
- **A game model cannot compensate for a small talent pool.** Everything in the SUP document about retention still outranks everything in this one. A brilliant spelidé applied to eleven fifteen-year-olds produces nothing.
- **The document is the easy part.** Writing this takes a weekend. Getting twenty volunteer coaches to say the same three words for ten years is the actual work, and there is no shortcut in this or any other document.
